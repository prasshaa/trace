### 1d. Entry-background stance (D23; job postings only)

A **posting-level** attribute. It records what one employer said about candidates' backgrounds in one posting at one
time. It is employer-specific **application eligibility** evidence (see §5): never a role requirement, and never a
permanent capability gap for a user. It is kept for the future hiring-transition stage (stage 4).

**Values:**
- `accepts_adjacent`: explicitly accepts adjacent backgrounds;
- `requires_target_role_experience`: requires existing experience in the target role;
- `excludes_specified_transitions`: explicitly excludes named transition backgrounds;
- `not_specified`.

### 2e. Disagreements to keep visible

1. **Titles don't reliably indicate level.**
   - J7 is titled "Principal" but its body says Senior PM.
   - J10 is titled "PM" but asks for 4–8 years.
   - J4's title names the domain.
2. **Entry from adjacent roles varies by employer** (§1d). This matters directly for Trace's users:
   - J11 explicitly accepts related data, analytics or engineering experience instead of PM years.
   - Eight postings require PM experience; J1 and J6 don't say.
   - J12 (Sarvam, closed) explicitly excluded transitions from consulting, project management and program management.

   This is application eligibility (§5, assessment 2), shown as attributed, dated variation between employers: never a
   rule, and never a capability gap.
3. **Analytics depth:** the frameworks ask for data-informed decisions; some postings ask for SQL.
4. **Go-to-market:** core in F3 and owned in J10; absent in F1 and in J11's internal data products.
5. **Experience and education:** no agreement between sources.
6. **AI:** frequent in postings and silent in the frameworks, so it is classed as emerging context.

| Element | What it holds |

| **Canonical requirements** | Role family and level, the class (core, context-dependent, differentiator, employer-specific), and context conditions |

## 5. Three separate assessments (D25)

The knowledge model, and the future capability assessment, keep three different questions apart. They are never
combined into one score or one generic gap list.

| Assessment | Question | Inputs | Output | Never |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Target-role capability** | What does the role generally require, and where does this user stand? | Canonical requirements (core, context-dependent when the user's context matches, Senior PM differentiators), with their source evidence; the user's confirmed profile | Per requirement: Demonstrated, Transferable, Gap or Unknown, with the profile records behind it | Uses employer-specific items (E1–E7) or entry-background stances as requirements or gaps |
| **2. Employer-specific application eligibility** | What did this employer, in this posting, say it wants from applicants? | One posting's stated criteria (E items) and entry-background stance (§1d); the posting's status and date; the user's profile | Per criterion, attributed and dated: matches the stated criterion, doesn't, or unclear. Only **current** postings are shown as opportunities | Counted as a capability gap; treated as permanent or universal; carried over to another employer |
| **3. Personal transition feasibility** | Which routes into the role are realistic for this user, and what would strengthen them? | The user's profile; observed routes and their evidence breadth (Phase 4, D4); the capability assessment (1); attributed employer examples (2), including employers that accept adjacent backgrounds and those that don't | A qualitative explanation per pathway (direct or intermediary), with reasons and their sources | A score, a probability or a share; evidence breadth used as suitability; one employer's restriction stated as "you can't" |

**Example:** a Business Analyst targeting PM.
- **(1)** shows where their discovery and prioritisation experience transfers, and the real gaps.
- **(2)** might show that Target's posting accepts adjacent data and analytics backgrounds, while a (closed) Sarvam
  posting excluded named transitions. Both are attributed and dated.
- **(3)** explains that some employers accept adjacent experience and others ask for PM experience first, and so
  whether a direct move or an intermediary role (such as Product Analyst) is the better route for them.

None of the three overrides another, and none is shown as a single number.
